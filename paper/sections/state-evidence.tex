\section{Does a learned native state provide useful history?}
\label{sec:native-state}
The completed R73 study changes the information entering the first FST,
rather than adding a temporal loss. It maintains a transported native
12-channel mean and, in the moment arm, an additional noncentral second
moment. The first square reads the squared mean plus a learned
channel-wise multiple of the nonnegative moment difference. This
empirical mixture dispersion includes texture, interpolation and motion
differences; it is not calibrated sensor-noise variance. Neither the
moment identity nor recurrent fusion is claimed as a new contribution.

Three independently optimized controls inherit the same 8,000-update
spatial checkpoint per seed and each receive 4,000 further updates on
the fixed mixed windows, using clipped frame MSE and four-frame BPTT.
Spatial and moment arms have 4,562 deployment parameters; mean history
has 4,550 because it omits the 12 moment coefficients. The spatial arm
retains current-frame adaptive sampling without past observations.
The moment/current evaluation disables history in the same trained
moment weights. This distinguishes retained history from changes in the
spatial mapping, while the independently trained mean arm tests the
incremental role of the second moment.

All six final folds, 200 new quality conditions, 240 new perceptual
comparisons and 20 fixed contact sheets complete normally. The contacts
were inspected separately after rendering. Combining the completed
plain references gives 280 joint conditions; Table~\ref{tab:native-state}
retains the target and every registered reference for both seeds and
domains. The 8K reference is the original spatial model; the 12K reference
matches the new arms' cumulative update count. Neither the six synthetic
conditions from three videos nor the four daytime ND sources constitute
independent final or natural nighttime testing.

Relative to its own current-frame path, native moment history lowers
temporal error by only 0.159--1.152\% across the four domain/seed means.
Relative to mean history, its PSNR changes by $-0.0032$ to $+0.0047$\,dB
and its temporal-error ratio spans 0.999795--1.000189. This numerical
proximity under the fixed pilot is not statistical or functional
equivalence. Against the matched 12K plain model with classical
recursion, temporal error is 37.1--50.8\% higher. The 20 registered joint
screens all fail, including the same-weight history and independent
moment tests. More parameters or a nonzero historical gradient did not
establish useful temporal information in this setting.

The two FP32 native feature states alone require 199,065,600 bytes at
1080p, excluding workspace and matching operations. Low parameter count
therefore does not establish a handset advantage. The fixed moment-state
search was stopped; no width, scale, coefficient, objective, learning-rate
or training-length grid follows these results. This does not rule out
other recurrent models, moment methods or longer training, and it does
not isolate a single cause of the limited historical effect.
